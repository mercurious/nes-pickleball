# Research: the sport (2026-10-10)

What the cartridge has to get right, digested from the USA Pickleball *Official Rulebook* (2026 edition, the
PDF as mirrored by Pickleball Canada), the sport's history pages, strategy guides and two videos. Rule numbers
are the 2026 rulebook's. The last section lists what was different in 1984, which is the era the cartridge
pretends to come from.

## 1. The era

| year | event |
|---|---|
| 1965 | Invented on Bainbridge Island, WA (Joel Pritchard, Bill Bell, Barney McCallum): a badminton net lowered to 3 ft, a perforated plastic ball, plywood paddles |
| 1967 | First permanent court (a neighbour's back yard) |
| 1972 | Pickle-Ball, Inc. formed to protect the game (the hyphenated spelling is the company's and a trademark; "pickleball" is the sport) |
| 1976 | First known tournament (Tukwila, WA) |
| **1984** | **The United States Amateur Pickleball Association (USAPA) organised; the first rulebook published (March 1984).** The same January, Nintendo released *Tennis* for the Famicom |
| 1984 | The first composite paddle is reported (Arlen Paranto, Boeing fibreglass honeycomb); wooden paddles still the norm |
| 1990 | Played in all 50 states |
| 2005 | USAPA reincorporated; the rulebook revised into its modern shape |
| 2020 | Rebranded USA Pickleball |
| 2021 | Let serves abolished (a serve that clips the net and lands in is played); the drop serve added as a provisional option |
| 2022 | Drop serve permanent; the "chainsaw" serve banned (release with one hand only) |
| 2026 | Rally scoring is a provisional option (Rule 14.A); mini-singles added (14.B) |

So the premise "the console is older than the sport" is not quite right: the sport is 18 years older than the
Famicom. The anachronism is cultural. In 1985 a pickleball cartridge would have been a regional curiosity that
only a Seattle-area licensee could have proposed. That is the fiction: a launch-window black-box sports title
for a sport whose rulebook was one year old.

## 2. Court and equipment (Section 3)

- **Court** 20 ft x 44 ft for singles and doubles alike (3.A.1); lines 2 in wide, measured to the outside edge.
- **Non-volley zone (NVZ, "the kitchen")** 7 ft x 20 ft on each side of the net; *all lines bounding it are part of
  the zone* (3.A.4.c). Its line is 7 ft from the net.
- **Centerline** from the NVZ line to the baseline, splitting each end into right and left **service courts**, which
  *include* their baseline, sideline and centerline (3.A.4.f). **Serving areas** are behind the baselines, bounded by
  the imaginary extensions of the sideline and centerline (3.A.4.g).
- **Net** 36 in high at the sidelines, 34 in at the centre (3.B.6, 3.B.7); posts 22 ft apart; the net is at least
  30 in deep (it does not reach the ground as a wall: a ball can go *around* the post, 13.C).
- **Ball** one colour, smooth, molded, perforated (indoor balls have fewer, larger holes); 2.87-2.97 in in diameter,
  0.78-0.935 oz; bounce 30-34 in when dropped from 78 in onto granite, i.e. a coefficient of restitution of
  0.62-0.66 (in 1984 the Cosom Fun Ball, a wiffle ball).
- **Paddle** at most 17 in long, length + width at most 24 in, rigid, no rubber or rough texture (3.D). In 1984:
  plywood.

## 3. Scoring and the serving sequence (Sections 1, 4, 5, 6, 14, 15)

- **Side-out scoring.** A point is scored only by serving and winning the rally (4.A). The first side to 11, leading
  by 2, wins (Section 1; tournaments also use 15 or 21, Section 15). Rally scoring (every rally scores) is a
  provisional 2026 option (14.A), not the game.
- **Singles (5.A).** Server's score even (incl. zero): serve from the **right** serving area to the opponent's right
  service court; odd: from the left. The server keeps serving, alternating sides after each point won, until they
  lose a rally (side out).
- **Doubles (5.B).** After a side out, the player standing on the **right** (by the team's score: the starting
  server is right when the team's score is even, left when odd; the partner the opposite) is **server 1**; the
  partner is **server 2**. Server 1 serves, alternating sides after each point won, until the team loses a rally;
  then server 2 does the same; the next lost rally is the side out. **At the start of each game the starting
  team has only one server**, treated as server 2: the game begins "zero – zero – two" (5.B.2, 6.B.2).
  Receivers never switch sides on their own; positions are fixed by the score.
- **Calling the score (6.B).** Before every serve, when everyone is in position: singles "server – receiver"
  (two numbers), doubles "serving team – receiving team – server number" (three). The *entire* score must be
  called before the serve is hit (6.C); serving during the call is a fault (6.C.1). The server must serve within
  10 seconds of the call (6.D). A wrong score can be challenged before the return of serve: replay (6.F.1);
  challenging a correct call is a fault (6.F.2).
- **Position errors (5.C).** Wrong server, receiver or position, caught before the rally ends: replay; caught
  after: the rally stands.

Worked example (doubles, A serves first): 0-0-2 → A wins the rally: 1-0-2, server switches to the left → A
loses: side out, 0-1-1 (B's right-side player is server 1) → B wins twice: 2-1-1 → B loses: 2-1-2 (partner serves
from the right, since 2 is even... note: the *starting* server of B is on the right when B's score is even; the
partner is on the left, and serves from wherever they stand) → B loses: side out, 1-2-1.

## 4. The serve (Section 7)

- **Feet** (7.A): at least one foot on the ground in the correct serving area; no foot touching the court or the
  surface outside the serving area at contact (foot faults).
- **Release** (7.B): one hand (or the paddle), visible, no spin added.
- **Volley serve** (7.C): paddle moving in an *upward arc*, the paddle head below the wrist, the ball below the
  waist at contact. Forehand or backhand. **This is the only serve of 1984.**
- **Drop serve** (7.D): drop the ball, hit it after the bounce; no height/wrist rule. 2021 and later only.
- **Placement** (7.E): diagonal to the opposite service court; the ball must **clear the NVZ** (landing on the NVZ
  line is out: 8.C) and land in the correct service court (its baseline, sideline and centerline are in).
  "With or without touching the net": since 2021 a net cord that lands in is played. Serve into the net, out,
  short, hitting the server's side: fault. A serve that hits the receiving team before landing: a fault on the
  **receiver** (the serving team scores), 7.E.5.
- **One serve.** No second serve exists (unlike *Tennis*'s double fault).

## 5. Rallies, faults and the kitchen (Sections 8-13)

- **Two-bounce rule** (10.A): the serve must bounce before the receiver hits it, and the return must bounce before
  the serving side hits it. Then anyone may volley (outside the NVZ).
- **Double bounce** (10.B): return before the second bounce. **Placement** (10.C): the ball must land in the
  opponent's end (lines are in, 8.B) or hit an opponent before landing. Into the net or out: fault. A ball that
  hits a player (except the paddle hand below the wrist): fault on that player (10.C.3).
- **A missed swing leaves the ball live** (10.E). Two partners both hitting one ball: fault (10.D.2).
- **Non-volley zone** (11.A): a volley must be *initiated* outside the NVZ. Touching the zone (incl. its line, or a
  dropped paddle, or a partner who is in it) while volleying: fault; **momentum** carrying you in after a volley:
  fault, even after the ball is dead (11.A.2); after standing in the zone you must get both feet out before
  volleying (11.A.3). Standing in the kitchen is fine for a ball that has bounced (the dink).
- **Net** (13): touching the net or posts while the ball is live: fault; hitting the ball before it crosses the
  plane: fault; crossing the plane *after* hitting is allowed (the "Erne"); around the post is legal (the "ATP");
  under the net or between net and post: fault.
- **Line calls** (8): players call their own lines; a ball that cannot be called out is in; "bounce it" between
  partners is communication, not a call (8.F.4).

Faults the cartridge can detect and name: OUT, NET, KITCHEN (volley from the zone / momentum), TWO BOUNCE
(volleying the serve or the return), DOUBLE BOUNCE, FOOT FAULT (serve), BODY (ball hits a player), SHORT (serve in
the kitchen), WRONG COURT (serve to the wrong service court), NET TOUCH. Umpire words of the era: "fault",
"side out", "point", "let" (1984), "game".

## 7. How the game is played (strategy, for the computer players and the chatter)

- **Serve deep** (the receiver is kept back); **return deep and come forward** to the NVZ line, where the point is
  usually won. The receiving team starts with one player already at the line.
- **The third shot** (the serving team's first hit after the return) decides the point's shape: a **drop** into
  the kitchen lets the serving team come forward; a **drive** at the opponents' feet or weaker side forces a
  weak reply. Coaches tell the non-hitting partner to watch the third shot: a good one, call "go" and advance;
  a bad one, call "back" and stay.
- **Dinking**: soft shots into the kitchen from the line, until someone lifts a ball too high; then the
  **speed-up** (a drive at the body or the gap). Resets (drops from the back) when attacked.
- **Lobs** over players stuck at the line; the partners then **switch** sides.
- **The middle** between two opponents breeds hesitation; the forehand player usually takes it; **stacking**
  keeps the stronger forehand in the middle (teams agree with hand signals: open palm switch, fist stay).
- **Erne** (volley from beside the kitchen, outside the sideline) and **ATP** (around the post) at the top level.
- Levels, as the computer: 1 stays back and pushes the ball over; 2 comes to the line sometimes; 3 drops and
  dinks; 4 speeds up high balls and covers the middle; 5 poaches, lobs, and never volleys from the kitchen.

## 8. What players say (the voice layer's vocabulary)

- **The score call** (the server, every serve): "four – two – one", "zero – zero – two"; singles "three – five".
- **Ownership**: "mine", "yours", "you", "me", "I got it", "help"; **after a lob**: "switch", "stay".
- **A ball going long**: "bounce it", "let it go", "no", "out"; and the call afterwards: "out", "good", "in".
- **Movement**: "go" / "back" (after the third shot), "up" (come to the line), "stay back" (the two-bounce
  reminder for the serving team: "let it bounce").
- **Between points**: "nice", "good get", "great shot", "sorry", "my bad", "side out", "second server",
  "nice serve"; the umpire: "fault", "side out", "let", "point", "game".
- Hand signals exist (open palm = switch, fist = stay); on the NES a small sprite above the player can stand in.

## 10. What the videos showed

- *How to Play Pickleball: The Ultimate Guide on Pickleball Rules* (Kyle Koszuta, 9:54): the two-bounce rule drawn
  over a real rally (serve bounces, return bounces, then volleys); the scoreboard overlay "SCORE 5 3 1" with the
  server number changing to 2 after a lost rally and the side out after that; the doubles rally footage shows
  both pairs at the kitchen line within three shots, dinking, with the server calling the score before serving.
- *Tennis NES* co-op footage (SL Gamers Argentina): the look the cartridge copies: a trapezoid court, the net a
  band across mid-screen, small same-size sprites far and near, the umpire chair at the net post, the score panel
  top-left ("Com"/"You"), two-colour court, a plain surround.
