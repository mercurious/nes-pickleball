# Findings 2026-10-11: building v0.0.1

The one-shot session for the cartridge, phase by phase (docs/PLAN.md §6). Hosts: the M1 for cc65 builds and
RetroArch (nestopia, through the UDP harness in `test/`), `etk-cloud` for MesenCE and the headless tests.

## Phase 0: the skeleton (gate met)

- cc65 2.19 (Ubuntu's build, copied from the node to `~/.local/cc65`; `CC65_HOME` set by the Makefile), our own
  kernel instead of neslib (`src/crt0.s`: reset, NMI with OAM DMA, palette and VRAM-buffer flush, scroll; VRAM
  helpers, DMC-safe pad reads, a 16-bit LFSR). One trap: cc65 force-imports `__STARTUP__`, which pulls the
  library's own NES startup (header, vectors and a 4 KB font) next to ours; the kernel exports the symbol itself.
- The court generator makes 103 background tiles including the 51-glyph font; the sprite tool packs 14 player
  frames, ball, shadow and two badges into 120 tiles (8x16 mode, table 1).
- RetroArch on Fedora: nestopia has no libretro memory map, so the harness reads with `READ_CORE_RAM` (the
  achievement address space, RAM at 0) and writes with `WRITE_CORE_RAM`; fceumm on the handhelds has the map.
- Screenshot: `docs/phase0_court.png`.

## Phase 1: ball, players, swing, serve (gate met)

- Positions are unsigned 8.8 fixed point (0-255.996 px); heights and velocities signed. The first build used
  signed ints and silently overflowed past 128 px (cc65 warned "condition is never true" on the clamps).
- The shot model computes the lift that lands a shot L px past the net for its speed, slows the shot until it
  clears the net, raises the launch speed by about T/512 for the drag (1/128 of the speed per frame), and pulls
  every shot half-way back toward the centre line (a straight shot from near a sideline leaves the trapezoid).
  The lateral direction comes from the swing's timing, as in *Tennis*.
- With the desktop reflex harness (`test/ra_play.py`) against the Phase 1 computer: serve, return, third shot,
  then a dink exchange at the kitchen line with volleys, past 20 shots (LONG_RALLY events at 10 and 20). The
  dinking game emerges from the physics: a low ball at the line cannot be driven over the net, so both sides
  push it softly until one lifts it.

## Phase 2: the rules engine (gate: the oracle, see the test results below)

- The state machine (PRESERVE, CALL, SERVE_DROP, FLIGHT, RALLY, DEAD, GAMEOVER) with the serving sequence from
  Rulebook Section 5: positions by each team's score parity, the first server of a rotation the player on the
  right, the starting server of a game as "2", side outs, and the umpire's calls for every fault the cartridge
  can detect (OUT, NET, LET, SHORT, WRONG COURT, FAULT for a serve not hit before it bounced, TWO BOUNCE,
  KITCHEN with the momentum rule, double bounce, a passed ball).
- The rules oracle (`test/oracle.lua`) is a Lua model of Sections 4-6 that plays exhibition games through the
  rally-result hook (mailbox byte $07FF) and checks the ROM's score, serving team, server number, server slot and
  the server's position on every score call. Its first version locked into a parity pattern in which the
  serving side always lost, which the score never survives (850 points in game one); the loser now comes from a
  linear congruential generator seeded in the test.

## Phase 3: the computer players (gate: the soak, below)

- Five levels: speed 0.94 to 1.45 px per frame, reaction 16 to 2 frames, depth error 10 to 2 px; a strategy tier
  per level (stay back and push; come in behind the return; drop the third shot and dink; speed up high balls and
  lob; aim at the gap). Doubles: the player nearer the ball's crossing point takes it, the forehand takes the
  middle, "bounce it" when the ball will still be 14 px high at the baseline.
- The first exhibition had the far team losing 11-0: the computer's targets were fixed screen offsets from the
  centre, which sit beside the near team's standard positions but outside the far team's (the far court is
  narrower on screen), so every near-team shot was a winner. The aim now seeks whichever of the opponents'
  middle and the two sidelines, at the landing row's width, is farthest from both; the lateral angle is capped
  at about 37 degrees; a level-3 exhibition then split 7 points to 6.
- Two arithmetic traps: the drop term of the height predictions, `(t*t >> 8) * 10`, was 25 % short at long
  flights (it fired "bounce it" on balls that landed in; now `((t*t >> 4) * 5) >> 3`); and a single "last shot
  seen" shared by all four players ran the team decision for one team only.

## Phase 4: modes, menu, HUD, sound (gate met: every mode plays through the menu)

- The title with the menu in *Tennis*'s asterisk style (mode, level, game to 11/15/21, one game or best of 3,
  lets replayed or played), pause with the pause tone, a demonstration match after 25 s idle, best-of-3
  continuation with the serve alternating by game.
- The APU driver: effects on pulse 1 and the noise channel, jingles on pulse 2 and the triangle. **The thwap**: a
  25 % duty pulse dropping from about 900 Hz to 700 Hz over three frames (volume 12, 8, 4, 2) under a burst of
  high noise (period 3 to 6, volume 10 to 2): the hollow plastic ball's pop over the plywood paddle's slap. The
  dink is the same at half the volume, one frame shorter. The bounce is a two-frame triangle "tock" with a
  whisper of noise; the net cord a low noise thud. Point, side out, fault and game have three-to-eight-note
  jingles with a triangle bass; applause is noise that crackles and fades after a long rally. The title tune
  is eight bars at 150 bpm. (Nobody has listened yet: a human verdict is owed.)

## Phase 5: the contract (gate met)

- Mailbox v1 at $07F0-$07FF (`docs/MAILBOX.md`), the RAM map generated from the linker's symbols
  (`docs/RAM_MAP.md`), 31 event codes, the serve gate, partner directives, speech bubbles (BG text over the
  court beside a player for 45 frames, put back from the court's own tiles), the rally-result hook.
- `sidecar/stub.py` against RetroArch on the M1: announced itself, answered ten score calls through the serve
  gate in the server's "voice", printed the lines, sent bubbles. A sidecar's announcement is kept across
  `match_init` so one started before the menu still owns the calls.
- RetroArch's `RESET` network command does nothing on the desktop build; `test/ra_restart.sh` relaunches it.
  The network remote loses taps sent right after a launch; the harness waits 2.5 s.

## Phase 6: the cartridge's own voice (gate met: the speech test)

- 25 words (zero to twenty, side out, fault, game, let) rendered by espeak-ng (`tools/speak.sh`, a voice as
  1985 as they come), trimmed, resampled to 7,046 Hz (DMC rate index 5), pre-emphasised and encoded by
  `tools/dmc.py` the way the DMC decodes (a 7-bit level stepping by 2 per bit): 10,017 bytes, in the DMC
  segment at $D800. The score call plays before every serve when no sidecar has announced itself, and the
  umpire says side out, fault, let and game. `test/speech.lua`: the DMC channel is active on 40 of the first
  call's 83 frames.
- PRG budget after everything: 20,862 bytes of code and data (1,666 free below $D800), 10,017 bytes of speech
  (217 free), 244 bytes of BSS, 79 of zero page.

## Phase 7: packaging

- MesenCE's test runner needs a `settings.json` with `Nes.Port1.Type = NesController` (a blank one shows the
  first-run wizard window forever, and with no controller `emu.setInput` is a no-op); `emu.setInput` only takes
  effect from the `inputPolled` callback; the sandbox has no `os` or `dofile`, so `test/run.sh` concatenates the
  library, the RAM map and the parameters into each test. About 200 frames per second on the node while the
  other project's build runs there.
- A clean clone of `main` builds byte-identical on the node and the M1 (`sha256` of the committed tree's ROM
  checked both ways).
