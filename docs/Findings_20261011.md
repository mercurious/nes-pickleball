# Findings 2026-10-11: building v0.0.1

The one-shot session for the cartridge, phase by phase (docs/PLAN.md §6). Hosts: the M1 for cc65 builds and
RetroArch (nestopia, through the UDP harness in `test/`), `etk-cloud` for MesenCE and the headless tests.

## Phase 0: the skeleton (gate met)

- cc65 2.19 (Ubuntu's build, copied from the node to `~/.local/cc65`; `CC65_HOME` set by the Makefile), our own
  kernel instead of neslib (`src/crt0.s`: reset, NMI with OAM DMA, palette and VRAM-buffer flush, scroll; VRAM
  helpers, DMC-safe pad reads, a 16-bit LFSR). One trap: cc65 force-imports `__STARTUP__`, which pulls the
  library's own NES startup (header, vectors and a 4 KB font) next to ours; the kernel exports the symbol itself.
- The court generator makes 103 background tiles including the 51-glyph font; the sprite tool packs 14 player
  frames, ball, shadow and two badges into 120 tiles (8x16 mode, table 1).
- RetroArch on Fedora: nestopia has no libretro memory map, so the harness reads with `READ_CORE_RAM` (the
  achievement address space, RAM at 0) and writes with `WRITE_CORE_RAM`; fceumm on the handhelds has the map.
- Screenshot: `docs/phase0_court.png`.

## Phase 1: ball, players, swing, serve (gate met)

- Positions are unsigned 8.8 fixed point (0-255.996 px); heights and velocities signed. The first build used
  signed ints and silently overflowed past 128 px (cc65 warned "condition is never true" on the clamps).
- The shot model computes the lift that lands a shot L px past the net for its speed, slows the shot until it
  clears the net, raises the launch speed by about T/512 for the drag (1/128 of the speed per frame), and pulls
  every shot half-way back toward the centre line (a straight shot from near a sideline leaves the trapezoid).
  The lateral direction comes from the swing's timing, as in *Tennis*.
- With the desktop reflex harness (`test/ra_play.py`) against the Phase 1 computer: serve, return, third shot,
  then a dink exchange at the kitchen line with volleys, past 20 shots (LONG_RALLY events at 10 and 20). The
  dinking game emerges from the physics: a low ball at the line cannot be driven over the net, so both sides
  push it softly until one lifts it.
