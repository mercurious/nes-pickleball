# Findings, 2026-10-24: building v0.0.2, the teammate add-on

The add-on of `docs/V002_BRIEF.md` (DESIGN.md §10, PLAN.md Phases 8-10), built in one session on the laptop
against the v0.0.1 cartridge in the desktop RetroArch (nestopia), with stand-ins for llamatalk's three servers.
No ROM change: `tools/addon.sh` refuses to pack a `pickleball.nes` that is not the v0.0.1 cartridge byte for byte
(sha256 `01b464bf…f674`). car8 was not reachable (neither by mDNS nor over its USB link); the maintainer then
granted car12, and the add-on ran there with the real servers: the exhibition twice and 1 PLAYER DOUBLES with a
stand-in and synthetic speech. What the maintainer's ears and hands still have to judge is at the end.

## What was found

1. **The cartridge does not clear `MB_MAGIC` on the title.** `docs/MAILBOX.md` promises 0 on the title and menu; the
   v0.0.1 ROM sets $5B at `match_init` and never clears it, so after a game (or the attract demo, which ends on
   any button) the title shows a stale mailbox: GAMEOVER after a game, any state after a demo. The contract's
   first sentence was never tested (the Lua tests start at a fresh boot, where RAM is cleared). The sidecar
   reads the title's own idle counter instead (`idle`, $04C7-$04C8: it moves every frame on the title and nowhere
   else) and holds the title to be up when that counter moved more recently than the mailbox did; a counter read
   that lands after a match began is overruled by the mailbox. Until that was right, the sidecar steered the menu
   into a live exhibition and START paused it. The fix in the ROM is one store in `title_screen` and belongs to
   the next cartridge (0.0.3, `MB_VERSION` kept at 1 since the meaning does not change); the contract doc now
   says what v0.0.1 does.
2. **The point's end is one frame.** `apply_point` raises POINT_WON (or SIDE_OUT / SECOND_SERVER) and then
   SCORE_CALL in the same frame, so a sidecar sees only SCORE_CALL (the brief's gotcha, confirmed: every point
   logs `missed_events: 1`). The sidecar derives the point from the mailbox instead: the score changed, so the
   serving team won; the serving team changed, so a side out; the server number changed, so the second server.
   The umpire says "side out" and "second server" from that.
3. **A read before the cartridge has booted looks like a match.** RetroArch answers `READ_CORE_RAM` before the
   ROM has cleared RAM; one such read carried $5B in the magic byte. The sidecar takes a mailbox for a match only
   when the version is 1, the state 0-6, the mode 0-4 and the level 1-5.
4. **One UDP read costs a frame.** RetroArch serves network commands once a frame, so a poll with one read runs at
   40-50 Hz, with two (the stand-in reads the zero page too) at 20-25 Hz. The mailbox poll is one read; the title
   counter is read every 0.4 s.
5. **The serve gate is comfortable.** With the fake voice (0.25 s a word) a three-word call released the serve
   1.2-1.5 s after the gate opened, against the cartridge's 4 s. Pocket TTS digits run 0.4-0.6 s, so about 2 s is
   expected on the handheld; the sidecar's own deadline is 3.2 s.
6. **autogamer's `ra.py` shadowed the sidecar's.** Both have one; the sidecar's has `poke` and a matching-address
   read. autogamer's app directory goes second on the path (for `talk.py`, `llm.py`, `hud.py`).
7. **autogamer's "no ROMs" rule needed a door for the author's own cartridge**: `check_no_roms.sh` prunes
   `pickleball/` in a release directory, and `release.sh` demands exactly one `.nes` in the tarball, that one.
8. **On the handheld the score call was late**: 2.7-3.2 s after the gate opened, four of sixteen at the sidecar's
   own 3.2 s deadline. Two causes: Pocket TTS renders a digit as 0.8 s of audio of which 0.3-0.4 s is the word
   (the rest silence), so a three-word call ran 2.6 s; and the umpire's "Out!" and a "Sorry!" were in the queue
   ahead of the call (the fault comes 75 frames before the gate). Now the score call goes to the front of the
   queue (a priority queue: the call 2, the umpire 1, chatter 0, and chatter that waited more than 2.5 s is
   skipped), cached phrases are trimmed of their silence when loaded (floor 120, 60 ms kept each side: a digit
   0.2-0.4 s), and the sidecar's deadline is 3.7 s against the cartridge's 4.0. After that: 21 gates in 0.6-2.3 s
   in the exhibition, 11 in 1.7-2.1 s in doubles, none at the deadline.
9. **The faithfulness guard rejected "Thanks!"** (it wanted two words); one word is an answer now. The second
   attempt, "You got this!", passed and was spoken live 2.4 s after the model answered.
10. **Qwen3-ASR heard every injected line right**: "Stay back.", "What is the score?", "Nice shot, partner.", "Your
    call." (a Pocket TTS voice resampled to 16 kHz put at `talk.py`'s inject path: no microphone in this session),
    3.0 s the first time, 0.6-1.2 s after.
